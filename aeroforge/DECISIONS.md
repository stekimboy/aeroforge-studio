# DECISIONS.md — ambiguity resolutions and fallbacks

## v2 — flying-wing specialisation

The product was narrowed to **flying wings only** at the user's request. The
binding contract for the rewrite is `SPEC_FLYING_WING.md`; these are the
judgement calls behind it.

- **Tailed configurations were deleted, not disabled.** Conventional, V-tail,
  twin-boom and canard are gone from `config_defs`, the optimizer, the CAD, the
  stability solvers (`solve_tail_aft` / `solve_canard`), the UI, the guidance
  and the tests. A configuration that is merely hidden rots and drags its
  assumptions (tail volume coefficients, fuselage sizing, wing-position solves)
  into every code path that touches it. Tests assert the concepts are absent.
- **The centre body IS the wing — one continuous full-span loft.** The old
  build was an ellipsoidal pod solid plus two wing lofts plus bolted fins,
  fused. It read as separate objects glued together because that is what it
  was. Real moulded wings (Skywalker X5, SonicModell AR Wing, Ritewing Drak)
  have no separate body: the same airfoil simply gets deeper and longer toward
  the root. The CAD now lofts one surface from tip to tip, with the section's
  thickness and chord scaled up over the inboard blend region and the leading
  edge extended forward at the centre. There is no fuselage solid to bolt
  anything to, so there is nothing to look bolted on.
- **Real proportions are a constraint, not a preference.** The old optimizer
  produced AR 5–6 wings with a root chord ≈ 0.20 × span, then needed a pod
  because the root was too thin to hold a battery. Measured airframes: the
  Skywalker X5 Pro is 1280 mm span with a 717 mm root (0.56 × span, AR 3.7);
  the SonicModell AR Wing Classic is 900 mm span with a 482 mm root (0.54).
  Each planform family therefore carries a `root_chord_frac_band` the optimizer
  is held to, and the centre body must be deep enough to actually hold the
  pack. This is the single biggest reason the old flying wings looked wrong.
- **Static margin band is 0.03–0.15, not the tailed 0.04–0.20.** Tailless
  aircraft are flown at lower margins; 20% of MAC on a wing is unflyably
  nose-heavy and would force an absurd amount of up-elevon trim drag.
- **Four planform families instead of one "flying wing" config**: `swept`,
  `bwb`, `plank`, `bell`. They are genuinely different aircraft with different
  sweep/taper/AR/washout bands and different vertical-surface answers, which is
  what makes five generated variants read as five aeroplanes.
- **The reflexed sections had to be rebuilt, not just extended.** The v1
  "RFX-9 reflexed" (`m=0.025, r=2.4`) carried **0.2% camber** — effectively a
  symmetric section, `alpha_L0 = -0.1 deg`, `Cl_max` 0.81, and a `Cm0` that
  flipped *negative* as Reynolds number rose. Positive `Cm0` is the single
  property a tailless wing's whole trim argument rests on, so this was a real
  latent bug, not a tuning preference. The library is now `m ~ 0.017-0.021,
  r = 1.45` (2.0-2.5% camber, crossing at 0.69 c, ~0.6% reflex droop) giving
  RFX-7/9/11 at `Cm0` +0.008…+0.022 and `Cl_max` 1.02-1.24 over Re 1.5e5-4.5e5,
  tuned against NeuralFoil to the stated target that the trim CG lands inside
  the tailless static-margin band.
- **The reflex invariant is checked on the GEOMETRIC reflex**, i.e. the
  thin-airfoil `Cm_c/4` (~ +0.04, Reynolds-independent), not the viscous `Cm0`
  — which genuinely goes negative below Re ~ 1.3e5 because the aft camber sits
  inside the separation bubble. The viscous value is still reported honestly,
  and `RE_MIN_HARD` was raised 5e4 -> 9e4 for the same measured reason.
- **Balance is bought with static margin and honest nose ballast, not with a
  wing-position solve.** A tailed model slides its wing fore/aft against the
  fuselage to place the CG; a flying wing has no fuselage and nowhere to slide
  anything. The two levers that remain are the static margin itself (kept
  inside the band, floored at 0.75x the mission target so handling is not
  traded away for a few grams) and ballast, which is added to all-up mass,
  penalised in the cost, capped at 15% and reported as `mass.nose_ballast_kg`.
  Pretending the balance is free would have made every weight number fiction.
- **Planks get a nose tractor motor, not a centre pusher.** An unswept wing's
  neutral point sits barely aft of its own quarter chord, so hanging the
  heaviest single item on the trailing edge forced 15%+ ballast. Electric plank
  practice (PW-51 class) is a nose motor; with it, plank ballast drops to
  12-185 g and every plank becomes feasible.
- **Where the 8-13 deg bell washout and the 0.15 static-margin ceiling
  conflict, the ceiling wins.** That much twist on a 25-deg-swept AR-6.5 wing at
  a cruise `CL ~ 0.2` trims at a margin above the band. Bell designs therefore
  sit at SM 0.150 with a small residual pitching moment (`cm_trim_residual`,
  up to ~ +0.05) carried on elevon trim and explained in `stability.notes`,
  rather than silently leaving the stable band. Every other planform trims
  exactly (residual <= 0.006).
### OpenCASCADE lofting lessons (found by building it wrong first)

- **OCC parameterises through-sections on the section INDEX, not on distance.**
  Bunching stations to "resolve curvature" therefore makes the surface bulge
  *between* them: a geometrically graded outboard segment put the bell
  planform's skin **104 mm behind its own trailing edge**, and an uneven
  LERX/panel split cost the plank 31 mm. Stations must be evenly spaced
  *within* a segment; extra resolution comes from adding SEGMENTS, not from
  clustering sections. (This inverts the naive reading of the v1 note about
  "clustered sections" — that note was about avoiding overshoot, and the fix is
  segmentation, not clustering.)
- **Segment boundaries must land where the section is C1.** A boundary placed
  where the planform blend is still curving leaves two lofts with genuinely
  different spanwise tangents and draws a hard panel line down each wing.
  Boundaries sit at the blend edge and the tip-start station, and the centre
  segment runs straight through y = 0 — splitting at the centreline gives
  mirrored, mismatched tangents, i.e. a crease down the spine.
- **Knife-edge trailing edges tessellate badly** (visible nicks along the whole
  TE) and are unbuildable anyway. Sections carry a finite TE of ~0.4% of local
  chord, floored at 0.8 mm.
- **`Shape.BoundingBox()` is not a measurement.** For B-spline faces OCC builds
  it from the control polygon and can report up to 0.3 mm outside the real
  surface, which produced a false "geometry ahead of the nose datum" failure.
  Envelope invariants are measured on the exported MESH.
- **The recorded envelope is measured, not estimated.** During the search the
  overall length/height come from a cheap analytic estimate (it is evaluated at
  several hundred grid points). That estimate was conservative by up to 38 mm —
  a plank reporting 209 mm tall while measuring 171 mm. Those two numbers are
  shown to the user as the aircraft's dimensions, so a conservative estimate is
  a WRONG number, not a safe one, and it also throws away designs that fit the
  box perfectly well. `optimize_design` now finishes by calling
  `cad.geometry.airframe_extents_m` (pure geometry, no OpenCASCADE, a few ms)
  and re-checks the box constraints against the true extents. Worst-case
  agreement with the built mesh is 3.5 mm, pinned by
  `test_recorded_dimensions_are_the_real_ones`.
  The import is deliberately deferred inside the function: `physics` must not
  depend on `cad` at module level.
- **A lofted surface bulges between its sections, and that has to be carried
  as an allowance.** The extents sampler is exact on the SECTION model, but the
  B-spline loft passes *through* those sections and overshoots slightly between
  them - up to 2.3 mm on a BWB, whose leading-edge root extension is the
  sharpest spanwise curvature of any planform. Taking more sample stations
  cannot fix it, because the deviation is a property of the surface rather than
  of the sampling. The recorded length therefore carries
  `max(3 mm, 0.6% of length)`. Height needs none: fin tops are already computed
  from the crown the fin is rooted on, which comes out conservative.
- **Fin tops must be computed at the station the fin actually stands on.** The
  extents helper evaluated every fin type's crown at 0.35 c — near maximum
  thickness, the highest crown on the section — while the builder puts a centre
  fin near the trailing edge where the crown has fallen away. That alone
  over-reported the plank by 13 mm.
- **One continuous surface, but a separable ASSEMBLY.** These two requirements
  pull opposite ways and both are real. The aerodynamic surface must be a
  single full-span loft — that is what stopped the aircraft reading as parts
  glued together — and the one-piece STL keeps it that way. But a CAD assembly
  has the opposite need: opening the STEP in Fusion and finding one monolithic
  body means you cannot export, edit or print anything separately. The parts
  export therefore CUTS the same surface at the spanwise station where the
  centre body blends out, giving `centre_body` + `wing_left` + `wing_right`.
  The pieces are coincident at the joint, so reassembling them reproduces the
  original surface exactly — nothing is approximated, only divided — and it
  doubles as the plug-in wing joint a wing this size needs for transport.
- **The separate canopy blister is gone.** It predated the hatch, and once the
  bay had a real lid the canopy was a second fairing on the same spine that
  corresponded to nothing a builder would make — it just showed up as a
  spurious extra body in the STEP export.
- **The hatch has to open INTO the bay, which took two fixes.** The first
  attempt left a membrane: the cavity ceiling stopped a fraction below the
  lid's underside, so lifting the lid revealed a thin skin still sealing the
  compartment. The second, and the real problem: the bay's ceiling is set by
  the crown at its OUTER EDGE, where the section is far thinner than on the
  centreline, so between that ceiling and the crown sat an 18 mm block of solid
  material — and a skin-thickness lid was only shaving the top off it. The
  aperture now removes the whole column down into the cavity, which also gives
  the electronics the full depth of the centre section.
  The test for this is precise rather than visual: a solid with a SEALED
  internal void reports two shells; one you can reach into reports one.
- **The motor mount is a faired NACELLE, not a disc on the trailing edge.** A
  flying wing's centre section is thin at the back — 9.3 mm deep on a 400 mm
  chord is typical — while the bolt circle for even a small outrunner needs a
  face about 37 mm across. The first version put a cone of that size on the
  trailing edge, so most of it hung in free air, it read as a part glued on
  afterwards, and it intersected the centre fin (which lives on the same
  centreline at the same station). What real pusher wings do instead is carry a
  faired nacelle, so the boss is now a loft that begins as the body's OWN local
  cross-section — entirely inside the skin, which is why the fuse leaves no
  seam — and morphs over a shallow taper (depth >= 2.2 x plate radius) into the
  circular mounting face. The face still sits flush with the trailing edge, so
  the mount never lengthens the aircraft.
- **A centre fin must clear the prop disc.** With a centre pusher the fin is
  slid forward until its trailing edge is ahead of where the nacelle leaves the
  body. That is not a modelling workaround: a fin sitting in the prop wash is a
  real design error, and the prop would strike it.
- **Handling qualities are now computed, because balance is not handling.**
  Every constraint in the optimizer used to be longitudinal or geometric: the
  app could tell you the wing balanced, and nothing about whether it would be
  pleasant — or even possible — to fly. `physics/handling.py` adds the other
  two axes on the same strip model: `Cn_beta` (weathercock stiffness, from the
  drag difference between the panels in a sideslip plus the fins), `Cl_beta`
  (dihedral effect, from the lift difference plus geometric dihedral), their
  ratio (Dutch roll versus spiral), and elevon authority against the moment
  actually needed to hold trim. Directional instability, spiral divergence and
  trim eating more than 75% of the elevon travel are now hard constraints with
  plain-English messages.
  Thresholds are calibrated for TAILLESS aircraft: a tailed model runs Cn_beta
  0.05–0.15/rad, a wing an order of magnitude lower and is flown that way
  quite happily, so judging one against tailed numbers just labels every design
  "weak". The split is the physics story and is asserted in tests — an unswept
  plank's stiffness is nearly all fin (0.0107 of 0.0114), a finless bell's is
  entirely sweep.
- **Proverse yaw is a response to ROLL INPUT, not to sideslip.** This was
  modelled wrongly first: a bell spanload does not give a Horten high
  weathercock stiffness — it is genuinely soft in `Cn_beta` (0.0025/rad here)
  and is flown that way. What the spanload buys is the SIGN of the yaw that
  comes with roll. Local induced drag changes by `alpha_i(y) * d(cl)`, and the
  bell's induced angle is quadratic, reversing at `eta = 1/sqrt(3) = 0.577`, so
  elevons outboard of that station sit in upwash: the down-going one LOSES drag
  and the nose swings into the turn. That is why the configuration needs no
  rudder and no fin (Prandtl 1933; NASA Prandtl-D). The reported quantity is
  therefore yaw-on-roll-input, and the tests assert the bell is proverse while
  a conventional spanload is adverse.
- **Hollowing the centre body: a hatch lid, and why every cut is now
  tessellation-checked.** The equipment bay is cut as a lofted cavity whose
  ceiling follows the crown and whose floor follows the keel, so the space is
  usable rather than a flat-topped box. The LID is not modelled: it is the cap
  the hatch prism selects out of the airframe, shelled against a copy of itself
  dropped by one wall thickness, so it inherits the crown's curvature exactly
  and comes out at skin thickness. Cutting that same shape back out of the
  airframe leaves an opening it fits with no gap either way.

  Two things had to be learned the hard way. A loft whose sections change WIDTH
  and HEIGHT at once twists and self-intersects, so the bay is constant-width
  and varies only in z; and the bay's width must be trimmed to where the body
  is actually wide - on a swept wing the forward end of the nominal bay sits
  ahead of the outboard sections, so taking the minimum available width across
  the whole nominal bay collapsed it to a 5 mm slot.

  Most importantly: cutting the bay, and scribing the lid outline, both
  produced solids that `isValid()` reported as fine and that OCC then refused
  to tessellate - the exported STL simply lacked everything forward of the bay
  (96% coverage, 73 mm of nose missing). That is the third distinct way OCC has
  silently returned a plausible-but-wrong result here, so cuts are now accepted
  only if the result still MESHES completely (`_tessellates_cleanly`), and the
  bay falls back through a ladder of narrower cavities before giving up.
- **Near-coincident faces make a boolean silently produce NOTHING.** The centre
  fin was placed with its trailing edge exactly on the wing's, which left two
  trailing-edge faces about 0.2 mm apart. OCC then computed an EMPTY
  intersection between two shapes that visibly overlap by thousands of cubic
  millimetres, and the fuse returned the fin as a second, disconnected solid.
  Note the failure mode: not an exception, not an invalid shape - a plausible
  result that is simply wrong, and one that only appeared at particular
  payload/cruise combinations. The fin is now inset `max(2 mm, 3% of its root
  chord)` ahead of the wing trailing edge, which is where a real one goes
  anyway: the trailing edge belongs to the elevon.
- **A tangent boolean makes a valid-looking solid invalid.** The hatch fairing
  originally let its rise fall to exactly zero at both ends, so it was tangent
  to the crown where it met it. OCC resolves a tangent boolean into degenerate
  faces: the fused shape stays ONE solid with the correct volume and still
  exports, but `isValid()` is false. It only showed on the deep BWB body, where
  the fairing barely protrudes (it added 3.9 cm^3 to a 17.6 dm^3 airframe). The
  fairing's ends now finish strictly BELOW the crown so it enters and leaves
  through real intersections, and it is skipped entirely when it would stand
  less than 2.5 mm proud - on a body that deep a real hatch is a flush panel,
  not a blister.
- **Washout is smoothed near the centreline.** A true `|y|` twist law puts a
  visible crease across the centreline on high-washout designs (bell, 11 deg),
  so `|y|` is replaced by `sqrt(f^2 + eps^2)`, renormalised so root and tip
  twist are exact — linear to within ~3%.
- **Chord and leading edge blend over a WIDER width than thickness.** Forcing
  the forward extension into the body half-width turns the nose into a beak,
  and chord and LE must share a blend width or the trailing edge picks up a
  W-shaped wobble. Thickness blends on `fb`; chord and LE on `1.6 x fb`.

- **An explicit vertical-surface choice outranks the variant character.** Each
  of the five characters carries a `vstab` knob as part of its look, and those
  knobs were merged OVER the request - so picking "centre fin" in the sidebar
  silently produced five sets of winglets. A character's `vstab` is now only
  its default: when the user selects an arrangement it is re-applied after the
  character's knobs, and `resolve_vstab` still filters it per planform (a BWB
  has no centre-fin option and falls back with a note; a bell-spanload wing
  always ends up with none).
- **`planform: "any"` is a UI state, not a family.** It is the sidebar default
  and means "I have not committed — show me the range". `to_optimizer_input`
  normalises it to `None`, so the optimizer only ever sees a concrete family
  and the reference character leads the gallery.
- **Bell-spanload wings carry no vertical surfaces at all.** The bell spanload
  (Prandtl 1933; NASA Prandtl-D) loads the tips so lightly that the tip region
  produces induced *thrust*, giving proverse yaw — the wing yaws into its own
  turn. That needs ~8–13° of root-to-tip washout, which is why the `bell` band
  is so much larger than the others. Fins on such a wing cancel the effect the
  configuration exists for, so `vstab_options` for `bell` is `("none",)` and
  both the optimizer and the tests refuse to put area there.

- **Python 3.12 instead of 3.11**: Python 3.11 is not installed on this machine (3.12/3.13/3.14 are). Used `py -3.12` — best binary-wheel support for cadquery/casadi. `run.bat` still prefers 3.11 when present, then falls back to 3.12/3.13.
- **Flexible requirement ranges resolved by pip**: exact `~=` pins caused a resolver conflict (cadquery vs numpy 1.26); requirements use `>=` floors and the tested resolved set is recorded at the bottom of README.md.
- **scipy-based optimizer with NeuralFoil polars**: AeroSandbox/NeuralFoil is used for airfoil polars (real low-Re aero). The design optimizer itself uses a structured feasible-region sweep + SLSQP polish (scipy) rather than CasADi end-to-end, because the mixed discrete/continuous problem (airfoil pick, config topology, battery cells) with box constraints is more reliably solved per-config this way; the spec explicitly allows scipy SLSQP / structured sweep.
- **Analytic polar fallback built in**: if aerosandbox/NeuralFoil import fails at runtime, airfoils.py falls back to thin-airfoil-theory + empirical low-Re corrections (all equations cited in code) with the same interface, so the app still runs.
- **Reflexed section for flying wings**: implemented as an analytically-generated reflexed camber line (cubic reflex term giving positive Cm0), because NACA 4-digit sections cannot produce reflex. Coordinates are generated, not copied from a database.
- **Watertightness check**: the exported BRep solid is verified closed/valid (`Shape.isValid()`), and the STL is verified to enclose a positive volume (numpy-stl mass properties) with >= 98% of tessellation edges shared by exactly two triangles. OpenCASCADE tessellates each BRep face independently, which leaves a small fraction (<1%) of zero-gap T-junction edges along fused seams — the surface is still closed (vertices lie on the shared curves) and slicers accept these STLs; a strict 100% edge-pairing test would false-fail on any OCC-exported boolean result.
- **Solid (not shelled) STL export**: shelling lofted thin airfoil solids with OpenCASCADE `.shell()` fails unpredictably on thin trailing edges. The exported model is a solid; printability is achieved via slicer wall/infill settings, and the weight model uses the matching effective printed density (see weights.py). Wall thickness for the build method is recorded in export metadata.
- **Canard volume target**: spec gives tail-volume bands only for H/V tails. For canards a canard-volume coefficient band of 0.15–0.35 is used (typical for stable canard RC designs); the canard is sized to lift in trim with CG ahead of the NP.
- **curl for health-wait in run.bat**: Windows 10 1803+ ships curl.exe; used for the launcher's health poll loop.
- **Size-normalized optimizer objective**: the cost is SPECIFIC cruise power (W/kg, ~ gV/(L/D·η)) rather than absolute watts — absolute power is minimized by trivially small aircraft. Two additional physically-motivated terms: a progressive penalty below Re ≈ 150k (real low-Re airfoil degradation at model scale) and a mild envelope-utilization preference toward ~88% of the box width (the box is the user's stated available envelope; larger models at the same wing loading fly better at model scale). Result: typical hobby proportions (e.g. 1.1 m sport, 1.4 m trainer) instead of 0.5 m micro designs.
- **Tail/fin roots buried into parent bodies**: all tail surfaces start at or below the parent surface (fuselage centerline, inside booms) so the fused export is exactly one connected solid — enforced by a `len(solid.Solids()) == 1` test for every configuration.
- **Propulsion specs removed; lumped power-system mass allowance instead**: AeroForge designs AIRFRAMES, so motor/ESC/prop/Kv/cell/battery sizing (and the thrust-to-weight and power-loading constraints) were deleted along with `physics/propulsion.py`. The airframe still needs an honest all-up mass, or stall speed and wing loading become fiction, so the motor(s) + ESC(s) + flight pack are carried as ONE documented allowance in `weights.py`: `pack = 0.045 kg x n_motors + 0.345 x (structure + payload)`, floored at 70 g (the smallest credible motor + ESC + LiPo at these sizes). The 0.345 coefficient is chosen so the pack lands at ~28% of all-up mass, inside the 25-32% typical of electric RC models. Because it depends only on structure and payload — pure functions of geometry, not of total mass — it is a trivially stable fixed point of the mass-convergence loop (it converges on the first pass and cannot oscillate). Default sport conventional: 777 g all-up with a 220 g (28.3%) power system, against 722 g with the old motor+ESC+battery sizing (which had bottomed out on its 45 g battery floor at 12% of AUW — unrealistically light).
- **Optimizer objective is SPECIFIC DRAG, not specific power**: with the power system gone, the old "specific cruise power" cost has no meaning. The objective is now `100 / (L/D)` = 100 x D/W at cruise — the power-free efficiency measure: the fraction of its own weight the aircraft must push, which is exactly what sets glide ratio, cruise thrust and range whatever power system the builder bolts on. It is size-normalized, so it does not bias toward trivially small aircraft. The x100 scaling puts it in the 5-17 range for real designs, comparable to the soft terms kept from before (wing-loading band centering, the Re ~150k floor penalty, the mild envelope-utilization span preference); hard-constraint penalties remain two orders larger and always dominate. Side effect: default sport conventional L/D rose from 9.6 to 11.5, because the old objective also rewarded low cruise speed through the propeller/motor efficiency chain.
- **Five design variants per request, physics-differentiated**: `physics/variants.py` runs the optimizer five times per request with different TARGETS and BAND WEIGHTS (static margin, aspect-ratio band, wing-loading band + its centering weight, tail volumes, dihedral, airfoil candidates, cruise speed, specific-drag weight, an explicit soft stall-speed goal). Every knob defaults to a no-op so existing callers and single-design tests are unchanged. The user's box and all safety constraints apply identically to every variant; an infeasible variant is returned anyway with its binding constraints, so the count is always five. Two design notes: (a) the Slow Flyer uses a LOWER aspect ratio, not a higher one — span is capped by the user's box, so the only way to buy the area that drops wing loading and stall speed is a shorter, fatter, high-lift wing; (b) the stall goal (0.72 x the balanced design's stall speed) is applied as a SOFT cost term rather than the hard `v_stall_target`, because the structural mass floor makes a 28% stall reduction physically unreachable at these sizes and an always-infeasible variant would be useless. Each variant is also seeded with its siblings' planforms (multi-start), so the Efficient Floater is never beaten at its own objective by a sibling that merely landed on a luckier grid point.
- **Tailless vertical surfaces are sized from real flying-wing proportions, not the tail-aft V_V band**: `V_V = l_V S_V/(S b)` with the 0.02-0.05 band is a TAIL-AFT correlation that assumes a fuselage-length moment arm. A flying wing's fin sits about one root chord behind the CG (l_V ~ 0.05-0.08 m at model scale), and solving that band for S_V demanded a centre fin of **24% of the wing area, 190 mm tall on a 187 mm root chord** — a sail that no flying wing has ever carried and that is genuinely hard to balance in yaw. What actually holds a tailless model straight is LEADING-EDGE SWEEP (in a sideslip the advancing panel makes more drag than the retreating one, yawing the model back), which is why swept RC wings (Zagi / Ritewing / Skywalker X5 class) carry 3-4% of wing area in small tip fins while an unswept plank, with no sweep to lean on, needs 6-7% on a centre fin. `stability.flying_wing_fin` reproduces those measured proportions, interpolating on sweep between 6.5% (unswept) and 3.2% (>= 25 deg), with a 1.15x penalty for a single centre fin (tip fins on a swept wing act on a materially longer arm). The resulting V_V (0.002-0.006) is REPORTED honestly rather than forced into a band that does not apply, and the guidance explains why so a builder does not "fix" it. Proportion guards clamp the fin against BOTH the chord it stands on and the semi-span — a chord-only floor demanded a 290 mm winglet on a low-aspect-ratio 1.4 m plank.
- **Flying-wing tips get flat trapezoidal fins, not blended winglets**: the earlier CAD grew the outboard panel into a smoothly blended, progressively canted airliner-style winglet along a circular arc. It was continuous and watertight but looked nothing like an RC flying wing — every foam wing on a field flies flat, straight, bolted-on tip fins. The tip is now a plain rounded wingtip with a separate trapezoidal fin whose trailing edge sits flush with the wing tip TE, built to the exact height/chord the physics sized.
- **Variants carry a SHAPE, not just a set of targets**: each character also sets wing taper, LE sweep and a fuselage body family (`classic` / `slim` / `deep` station tables in `cad.geometry._FUS_STATION_SETS`) — a trainer's near-rectangular wing and deep cabin, a sport model's sharply tapered swept wing and slim long nose, a sailplane's strongly tapered slender wing. These are real design choices that feed straight back into the physics (MAC, neutral point, induced drag, structure mass), and they are what makes the five results read as five different aeroplanes rather than one aeroplane at five sizes.
- **The size-box overlay is measured from the belly, not the centreline**: `height_total` is measured from the lowest point of the airframe upward, so drawing the box centred on z = 0 put half of it underground and made any tall fin appear to burst out through the top of a box the aircraft genuinely fitted inside. The wireframe now sits with its floor on the model's lowest point, matching how the constraint is evaluated.
- **Lazy variant previews**: physics for one variant takes ~0.4 s but its CadQuery solid takes 70-130 s (it was 2-6 s when this was written, before the hollow equipment bay, the blended nacelle and the print-in-place hinges - all of which are boolean work on a large spline skin), so building all five up front would put the better part of ten minutes in front of the user. `POST /api/generate` runs the physics for all five and builds only the PRIMARY variant's preview (main view renders immediately); `GET /api/preview/{id}.stl` builds and caches the rest on first request, under a per-id lock with an atomic temp-file rename, so the frontend can prefetch the other four concurrently without meshing the same solid twice or serving a half-written STL.
- **Control surfaces are cut free and hung on print-in-place hinges, and the clearance comes from the BEVEL, not from a parallel gap**: the first separated-elevon build interleaved five cylindrical knuckles along the hinge line and bored them for a 1.5 mm music-wire pin. Two things were wrong with it. First, it was not print-in-place at all — the builder still had to thread a wire, and the bore was the only thing holding the surface on, so straight off the bed the elevon simply fell off. Second, and worse, it could not move: the surface arrived square-nosed from the split, and a square nose on a centre hinge binds against the wing trailing edge at about **one degree** of deflection (measured: 14 mm³ of interference at 5°). The parallel `HINGE_GAP_MM` gap the old code cut did not help, because the binding is a rotation problem, not a spacing one. `backend/cad/hinges.py` replaces it with the arrangement real RC practice uses: the standard **double bevel** on the surface leading edge, apex ON the hinge axis and truncated to a 0.4 mm flat, with a matching **cove** cut into the wing trailing edge on the same axis. Because the cove is rotationally symmetric about the axis it clears the surface equally at every angle, so it never becomes the limit; the bevel's own half-angle is what sets the throw, and 45° buys **±41° geometric** against the ±25–30° an elevon is ever asked for. The pin now belongs to the wing, runs continuously through the surface's barrel, and is printed in place with 0.30 mm radial clearance (top of the 0.2–0.3 mm PLA window, because the barrel prints axis-horizontal and the bore crown is a bridge whose first layer sags). Hinge count dropped from five bunched knuckles to **two spaced at 20%/80%** of the hinge line — Model Aviation's "a hinge every 4 in on the ailerons" scaled to model size, hung near the ends so the outboard end cannot flutter. Barrels are sized to the LOCAL section (≤0.88 × depth, hard cap 5 mm) instead of one size taken from the thinnest station: on the user's 500×610×350 swept FPV wing that is 5.0 mm OD at the 33 mm station and 4.05 mm at 132 mm, where the old code put 7 mm knuckles on a 5 mm section. A station too shallow to house a barrel WALKS toward the thick end in 4%-of-span steps rather than being dropped, and only if there is nowhere to walk does the module fall back to bevel-only — which is still a legitimate tape-hinged surface, just not a pinned one. Knuckle pockets are flat-bottomed **mortises**, not cylindrical clearance pockets: a cylindrical pocket left a 0.3 mm sliver of skin over the knuckle crown on a 4 mm section, thinner than one extrusion width, which the slicer either drops or prints as stringing. Every clearance in `HINGE_DEFAULTS` carries the published source it came from, so none of them is a modelling fudge.
- **The equipment bay and its canopy are a module, and the canopy is a TRAY LIFTED OUT OF THE SKIN rather than a modelled cap**: the first hollow-body build cut a cavity, cut a prism through the crown to open it, and called the piece that came out the lid. On a real optimized wing that lid was **3.7 cm3 - an 81 x 48 mm scrap of skin** - and the user reported the canopy as "missing completely" from the STEP. It was not missing; it was too small to see and useless as an electronics hatch. `backend/cad/hatch.py` rebuilds it as the thing an FPV wing actually has: a compartment with real bulkheads, an aperture whose floor sits BELOW the lowest point of the bay ceiling (so opening it always breaks through into the compartment instead of peeling a membrane off the top of it - the earlier build did the latter, and the bay had no access point even with the lid removed), a seat lip left standing inside the rim, and a canopy that drops onto that lip on a skirt `4 x wall` deep. On the same wing that is now a **12 cm3 canopy over a 180 cm3 bay** with a usable 83 x 45 x 22 mm box inside it. Clearances are the FDM box-lid figures - 0.35 mm per side (Snapmaker's 0.3-0.5 mm clearance fit), 0.20 mm seat drop so the canopy lands flush-to-slightly-recessed and never proud - plus 6 x 3 mm magnet pockets, which is how the X8-class hatches are actually retained. Three things were learned the hard way and are worth not re-learning. (a) **The seat is left behind, not built and fused on.** A shelf modelled as a slab of constant vertical thickness becomes a sub-millimetre sliver where the crown is steep, and OCC's spline booleans return solids of NEGATIVE volume there - measured on a 1.1 m BWB, a seat that should have been 12 cm3 came back as -248 cm3. The compartment is instead assembled from three chunky pieces and the seat is simply the material none of them claim. (b) **Both canopy surfaces are taken from the airframe itself**, dropped by the skin thickness and the skirt depth. Cutting the underside with a band interpolated through sampled crown heights let the band ride ABOVE the real lofted skin between samples, punching windows through the top skin and severing the skirt; the result was still a closed, valid, correctly seating single shell that passed every test, and only the canopy's volume swinging 29 -> 64 cm3 with loft resolution revealed it. (c) **The seat clearance is measured along the surface NORMAL**, not vertically: on a near-vertical crown a 0.20 mm vertical offset gives essentially no clearance normal to the surface, which is why the BWB canopy touched its seat at 0.000 mm before this was fixed.
- **Two different mesh tests, and both are needed**: `hatch` gates every ladder rung on "did OCC triangulate every face" AND on total meshed area / BRep area >= 0.985. The per-face test is the right question for a 12 cm3 canopy, where one 6 mm magnet pocket at the meshing chord tolerance is ~2% of the area and an area ratio false-fails a perfectly good part. But OCC will also happily mesh every face and still lose most of a BIG one, and that is what puts a hole in the exported skin - it once cost 73 mm of nose, and while this integration was in progress it shipped an airframe at 96.9% coverage with 51 mm of the aircraft simply absent from the STL, which `isValid()` and the per-face test both called fine. Area ratio is the only thing that sees it. Both run, per rung; there is deliberately no retry ladder wrapped around the module, because a ladder outside a ladder made a single wing cost 288 s.
- **An inboard fin must not stand on a moving control surface**: `twin_fin` puts its fins at 58% of the semi-span, and the default elevon band starts at 35%, so the fin sat inside the elevon's span with its root chord straddling the hinge line. The CAD fuses fins into the wing first and then cuts the elevon out from under them, which handed back a **115 mm tall, 236 cm3 "control surface" with a fin on top of it** - a part that would have deflected the fin along with the elevon. Real practice settles it: on a Skywalker X5 the two fins sit inboard and the elevons run OUTBOARD of them. `optimizer.elevon_layout` therefore moves the elevon's inboard end outboard of any fin station plus a 4%-of-semi-span clearance (0.35 -> 0.62 on that wing), and it does so in the PHYSICS rather than clamping it in the CAD, because the elevon's span is what sets its roll authority and the handling check has to see the same surface the builder gets. Elevons on that design went from 236 cm3 and 115 mm tall to 63 cm3 and 11 mm tall.
- **The control horn solves its own station from the servo arm's, so the pushrod cannot be diagonal**: the first servo integration put the horn at `station_frac=0.5` - the middle of the elevon - and asked `servo_bay` for a servo CENTRE station. But the arm does not sit at the servo's centre; it sits on the spline, near one end of the case, 11.65 mm outboard of mid. The two were therefore placed independently, and on the user's 610 mm swept wing they landed **64.6 mm apart in span**. A pushrod is a straight piece of wire: with the ends 64.6 mm apart across a 62 mm chordwise reach the rod runs at nearly 45° to the plane of both arcs, which converts part of every command into a side load on the servo's output bushing, makes up-throw and down-throw unequal, and reams the horn hole oval until the resulting slop turns into flutter. The sources are unanimous that this matters and none of them publishes a tolerance for it - HobbyKing's install guide says servo arms "are not designed for side forces, and a side force may twist or fracture the horn causing an inflight failure"; Model Aviation says "an off-axis pushrod will eventually ream out the hole in the control horn and create a sloppy connection"; Great Planes says the free play is then "greatly magnified, possibly causing destructive control surface flutter". In CAD the answer is not a tolerance but a construction: `servo_bay` takes `arm_y_mm` and places the CASE so the ARM lands on a given plane, `control_horn` takes `align_world_y` and solves its own station on the hinge line to match, and the residual is measured and reported rather than assumed. It is now **0.000 mm**, and the built-in radii (`arm_r_mm` = `r_design_mm` = 11.0, the outer hole of a 9 g arm) make the four-bar a true parallelogram: ratio 1.000, ±45° of servo giving ±45° of elevon. Two things fell out of doing it by construction. The sign of the case offset has to be taken about `sgn*half`, not `half` - dividing by span alone got the right wing right and mirrored the offset the wrong way on the left, which was worth 15 mm of asymmetry between the two servos. And the "skew" the linkage solver already reported is measured along the HINGE axis, so on a swept wing it reads the sweep itself as misalignment (4.9 mm on a wing whose holes shared a plane exactly); what actually decides whether a rigid wire binds is how much the END-TO-END DISTANCE varies through the throw, which is now computed in 3-D by rotating each end about its own true axis. It is **0.035 mm** over the full ±45°, and the 4.8° between the hinge axis and the shaft axis is simply not worth chasing.
- **The pushrod hole is a keyhole, because the two jobs it has want different diameters**: a hole sized to PIVOT 1.0-1.2 mm music wire is ~1.5 mm, and a hand-bent Z-bend will not thread it - builders report heating the wire and forcing it through. A hole sized to accept the bend is ~2.5 mm (roughly 2× the wire), and leaves ~1 mm of lash, which Model Aviation identifies as exactly the start of the flutter chain above. Du-Bro's own answer is to dodge the problem: put the Z-bend at the SERVO end, where the arm unscrews from the spline, and a removable E/Z link at the horn. That is not available here - this horn prints as part of the elevon and the builder is bending the wire by hand - so the hole is both sizes at once: a **2.5 mm feed lobe offset 1.6 mm radially outboard of a 1.5 mm running seat**, overlapping into a **1.14 mm waist** that a 1.2 mm wire has to snap past. Thread the bend through the lobe, pull the rod outboard, and it is captive in the seat; the rod then loads the seat tangentially, across the slot rather than along it, so it cannot walk back out. The blade tip had to grow with it - the lobe reaches `offset + d/2` past the deepest seat, and leaving the tip at the plain `hole_edge_mm` would have broken the lobe straight out through the end of the horn.
- **The servo pocket holds the servo, not the arm's swept volume**: the pocket used to carve out the arc the arm sweeps, reaching 22 mm forward of the shaft, which drove the fit search inboard hunting for a section deep enough to roof it. That volume buys nothing: the arm is fitted AFTER the servo drops in and works below the skin in free air, tangent to the lower surface, which is how a recessed foamie servo is actually rigged - the Skywalker X8 manual has the horn standing proud of the skin with the pushrod running externally to the elevon horn. What the pocket owes the arm is a number rather than a cavity, so `servo_bay` now reports `arm_free_deg`: how far the arm can swing before its tip climbs back above the keel line into wing material. On the test wing that is **38° aft / 72° forward**, against the ±20° of elevon an X5 is flown on, and `servo_bay` warns if it ever falls short. The pocket lost 1.3 mm of chordwise extent and the whole 22 mm forward well.
- **The servo lead leaves from a corner of the case, so the channel starts at that corner**: the lead channel was centred on the pocket's chordwise centreline, which is about 11 mm from where the wire physically emerges - so the wire had to double back on itself inside the pocket before it could enter the run. The lead exits one short END face of the case, low down, and on the SG90/FS90 case that is the end the output shaft is offset toward. This is the one dimension on the part that nobody draws: FEETECH, TowerPro and AUS Electronics all omit the lead from their dimensioned drawings, so the face and height come from calibrated measurement of manufacturer product photography (Adafruit 169 and 2442, TowerPro's SG92R catalogue shot) and clones genuinely move it. The design answer to that uncertainty is not a better guess but a tolerant geometry: the channel mouth goes at the measured corner, and the stub is left **open to the lower surface** like the pocket it leaves, so a lead emerging anywhere along the pocket floor still lies in the run. That also removes a bottleneck - a JR/Futaba plug is ~8 × 6 mm and will not thread the 5.6 × 4.2 mm stub, and the X8's own manual has the builder widening exactly this slot by hand for that reason. An open channel with the lead laid in and covered afterwards is what moulded foam wings do anyway.
- **STEP export was never broken; the browser was throwing the file away**: reported as "clicked it several times with no output". The endpoint returns **HTTP 200 with a 28 MB assembly** - it just takes **161 s**, because `export_step` calls `build_design_parts` and nothing cached it, so every click paid for a full CAD build and concurrent clicks fought each other for the same cores. On top of that the download itself was being cancelled: the frontend created an object URL, clicked a **detached** anchor, and called `URL.revokeObjectURL` synchronously on the next line, which for a blob this size revokes the URL before Chrome has finished reading it - the fetch succeeds, the button re-enables, and no file ever appears. Fixed at both ends: the anchor is appended to the document and the URL is revoked on a 60 s timer, the button counts the seconds so a two-minute build does not read as a hung control and a banner confirms the saved filename and size, and `/api/export` now caches one built file per (design, format) under the same lock as the exploded preview, so the second click is instant.
- **A servo may not walk inboard past the surface it drives**: `servo_bay` walks INBOARD looking for a section deep enough to roof its pocket without breaking the crown, which is right on its own terms and wrong as a whole. On a thin outer panel it walks clean out from under the control surface: on a 1.07 m bell wing the arm landed **32 mm inboard of the elevon root**, the horn clamped to the surface's own inboard end, and the two finished **91 mm apart** - a pocket that cannot drive its own surface with a straight pushrod. The walk is now floored at the elevon's inboard station. Constrained that way the bell refuses, and the refusal is the useful output: the deepest usable section anywhere under that elevon is **11.0 mm** against the **14.0 mm** a 9 g servo needs (12.5 body + 0.35 clearance + 1.2 roof), so an HXT900 genuinely does not fit there - a real property of a thin Horten planform, not a modelling failure. The message names the number and the way out (a servo no thicker than 11 mm; the 5 g class is about 8 mm) and the elevon still gets its fused horn, so the builder can rig whatever servo they choose. Worth noting that this was found by the alignment test rather than by inspection: the geometry was valid, watertight and passed every other check, and only the arm-to-horn residual showed that the linkage was nonsense.
- **Export is asynchronous: start + poll + download, never one long fetch**: the first export fix (in-document anchor, deferred revoke, response caching) was necessary but not sufficient, and the proof came from driving the user's actual browser: with the five variant previews building alongside it, the STEP build held its HTTP request open for **7+ minutes** - the synchronous fetch is a race against every timeout between the browser and the server, and losing it looks exactly like "clicked it several times with no output" because the server-side build then completes anyway and writes a file nobody delivers (a finished 23.3 MB STEP was sitting in exports\ from precisely such an orphaned request). `POST /api/export/start` now returns JSON immediately and hands the build to a daemon thread keyed on (design, format); the frontend polls `/api/export/status/{id}/{fmt}` every 3 s (each request fast, none killable by a header timeout, transient failures swallowed and retried); and the finished file is fetched by plain anchor navigation to `/api/export/file/{id}/{fmt}`, so the browser's own download manager owns the transfer and there is no blob, no object URL and nothing to revoke. The build also now survives the tab: close the page mid-build and the file still lands in the cache, and the next click returns it instantly. The synchronous `POST /api/export` stays for scripts and the test suite (TestClient has no timeout), with a docstring saying why the browser must not use it. Measured in the user's browser: start 200 in milliseconds, 46 status polls over 465 s while previews competed for cores, green banner, 23.9 MB in Downloads.
- **The horn keyholes are bored LAST, and every bore is PROVEN open**: parts reached the field with the horn triangles solid - no pushrod holes at all - while every build step had reported success. The boring loop cut the standalone horn before the fuse and kept the cut only if OCC returned one valid solid; when the cut failed, the loop silently kept the unbored horn, and nothing anywhere ever checked that the FINISHED part still had its holes. The measured facts made it stranger: byte-identical code bored clean holes when run in a plain process and lost them inside the server's build (probed by ray-casting the served preview STLs - three designs, both elevons, zero enclosed voids in any horn silhouette), so this was environmental flakiness in a boolean, exactly the class of failure that must not be trusted to a status code. `control_horn` now (a) fuses the unbored horn into the surface FIRST, so no later boolean can eat the holes, (b) bores each keyhole into the fused solid, (c) verifies each bore by point classification at the seat AND the feed lobe - the material must actually be gone - and (d) on failure retries with the two cylinders cut separately (the simpler boolean) before reporting a warning that names the depth and tells the builder to drill it by hand. `info["holes_cut"]` records the truth per hole. The general lesson repeats the mesh-gate one: `isValid()` says a shape is well-formed, not that an operation did what was asked - every cut whose absence matters needs its own existence check.
- **Wire runs end INSIDE the bay void, aimed at the void the hatch actually carved**: the motor channel was driven 6 mm past the bay's NOMINAL aft wall (`bay_start + bay_length`), but the hatch ladder shrinks the cavity about its centre, so the real aft bulkhead can sit 20+ mm forward of the nominal one - the channel then dead-ends inside solid material, a wire run that walks up to the compartment and never enters ("the wire leads to it but never actually enters", user-reported). `build_bay` now records the void's world-x extents (`bay_mm["x0_mm"]/["x1_mm"]`) and both the motor run (aimed at `x1_mm` + overshoot) and the servo runs (ended 6 mm INBOARD of the void's side wall at its actual mid-length) target the carved void. Overshooting into the void is free - there is no material there - and the mesh gate in `cut_conduits` still guards every cut.
- **The servo pocket's boss patch gives the fitted horn hub wiggle room**: removing the arm well entirely (right for the blade, which swings in free air) also stripped the clearance around the OUTPUT: the boss patch had shrunk to the bare 6 mm moulded collar, so the 8.5 mm hub of a fitted horn - which sits at skin level and turns with every command - would scuff the pocket edge on the first input. The patch is now hub diameter + 3 mm of swing room each way, chordwise, and hub-deep: wiggle room, not a cavern.
- **The horn hole line scales AFT toward the trailing edge, and the horn grows DEEPER with it (user request: leverage)**: the hole line was a fixed 3.2 mm aft of the hinge axis; it now sits at 30% of the surface's own aft reach (capped at 12 mm, clear of the thin TE). The physics of what that buys had to be measured rather than assumed: for the near-chordwise pushrod this linkage runs, the moment arm about the hinge is the perpendicular from the hinge axis to the ROD LINE - essentially the hole's DEPTH below the axis - so aft offset alone adds no torque, only RAKE, and past ~50 deg of rake the four-bar approaches a toggle: a 62 deg horn on the 610 mm wing could not reach +10 deg of surface at ANY arm clock. The hole depth therefore grows with the aft offset so the rake never exceeds 35 deg (`rake_max_deg`), which lands the holes at 21-24 mm from the axis - Du-Bro Micro Razor scale, sold into exactly this class. Against the 11 mm arm of a 9 g servo the mechanism is geared DOWN (ratio 0.57-0.63: about +/-26 deg of surface for +/-45 of servo, roughly 1.9x the holding torque of the old 1:1) - more force, less movement; you cannot have both from the same servo, and the linkage report says which trade was made. With the hole no longer under the hinge axis the parallelogram trick stops applying, so `linkage` SEARCHES the arm's neutral clock over the whole useful circle for the angle that balances up-throw against down-throw AND penalises any throw below the ~25 deg an elevon flies with (without the second term the 'most balanced' clock can be one that jams the rod early and balances two uselessly small throws - measured, again). `_install_servos` runs that solve per elevon and ships it in `meta["servos"]["linkage"]`; the builder sets the arm to the reported `arm_neutral_deg` at the bench.
- **The bay cut is verified to have actually hollowed the body, because a boolean can fail without failing**: on a 1.2 m deep-crowned body, `airframe.cut(void)` returned a valid, single-shell, cleanly-meshing solid in which the requested 249 x 95 x 55 mm compartment simply was not there - the only material removed was a millimetres-thin lick along the cavity floor. Every existing gate passed (validity, shell count, mesh coverage ARE all satisfied by an airframe that was never hollowed), the bay module reported success with the full planned dimensions, `test_centre_body_is_hollow` read those reported dimensions and passed, and the user found out from the printed part - the motor wire "leads to the bay but never actually enters" because there was nothing to enter. The void SOLID was blameless: built in isolation it is a perfect 1519 cm^3 compartment in exactly the right place; only the subtraction quietly did nothing. `_attempt` now probes six points spread across the intended cavity in the CUT RESULT (they must classify as air), retries the cut once on `.copy()` operands, and otherwise rejects the rung so the ladder tries a different shape. On the failing design, rung 0 fell to the mesh gate, rungs 1-2 were caught leaving the compartment solid - rung 1 is the one that had been shipping - and rung 3 (0.88 length, 0.72 width) cut correctly and verified hollow: a 239 x 76 mm bay that exists beats a 249 x 95 mm bay that does not. This is the third instance of the same lesson (mesh gate, horn keyholes, now the bay): an OCC status code says the RESULT is a well-formed solid, never that the operation DID ITS JOB - every operation whose absence matters needs its own existence check.
- **The motor wire run aims at MEASURED void air, in all three axes**: `x1_mm`/`x0_mm` (recorded by the hatch from the stations it actually carved) fix the aft-wall x; the z band is measured by `geometry._void_z_band` - a classifier ladder down the BUILT airframe at the void's aft wall - because every model of that number lied in turn: the nominal `bay_start + bay_length` was up to 20 mm aft of the real wall, the hatch's planning profiles put the ceiling at +25 where the carved air started at -13, and its `cavity` solid is the compartment CONCEPT (it reaches up through the hatch aperture) rather than the hole in this airframe. `motor_conduit` takes the measured band as `z_end_mm` and dives to it over the last 40% of the run (`ceiling - 6`), so the mouth opens into the compartment's actual air. Verified on the deep 1.2 m body: channel exit (268.6, 0, 12.9) inside the measured band (-10.4 .. +18.9), connectivity visible on the section map.
- **The horn hole is ONE plain 2.5 mm circle, by the builder's decision**: the keyhole (1.5 mm running seat + 2.5 mm feed lobe, defended above on flutter-lash grounds) read on the printed part as "two circles put together", twice per horn, and the builder asked for a single clean 2.5 mm hole per horn. Their linkage, their call - recorded so the keyhole is not "restored" as a fix. The engineering trade is stated in the module docstring: 2.5 mm over 1.2 mm wire leaves ~1 mm of play, so the lash mitigation moves to the bench (tight Z-bend or an E/Z-style keeper, which is Du-Bro's own pattern). The keyhole geometry survives behind `feed_d_mm`/`feed_offset_mm` for anyone who wants it back, `n_holes` dropped to 1, and the bore-verification machinery (bore last, classify open, retry, warn) applies unchanged - the test now asserts exactly one verified-open 2.5 mm bore.
- **The motor leads enter through an open 6.5 mm BELLY PORT, not an internal channel - builder's decision**: the internal run from the mount face to the bay (teardrop section, routed through the spine, dived to the measured cavity band) is replaced by `geometry._motor_port`: a vertical 6.5 mm bore through the bottom skin just inside the bay void's aft end on the centreline - the nearest hollow point to a pusher motor - cut from free air below the keel up through the cavity floor. The leads run externally along the belly from the motor and in through the hole, which is how pusher foamies are actually wired, and the hole prints cleanly in the first layers where the old enclosed channel needed its self-supporting roof. Aimed with the same MEASURED numbers as before (`x1_mm` from the carved stations, floor from `_void_z_band`), cut through `cut_conduits` so it stays mesh-gated, and tested in the geometry: a point that used to be skin on the bore's axis must classify as air. `conduits.motor_conduit` remains in the module, unused, for any future return to internal routing.
- **Horn hole rim thickened and the horn pushed further aft (builder feedback, round 2)**: the 2.5 mm hole's rim was ~1.4 mm from the blade edge ("very thin and may break off") - `hole_edge_mm` 2.6 -> 4.0 leaves 2.75 mm of wall on every side of the bore, and because the blade tip grows with the margin the hole also sits visibly higher in the triangle, which the builder asked for. The hole line moved from 30% to 42% of the surface's aft reach (cap 12 -> 16 mm) for more moment arm, and the rake cap rose 35 -> 45 deg - the builder wants aft AND high, which IS a higher-rake horn; the 62 deg toggle-jam stays away and the harness measures -25/+31 deg of surface for +/-45 of servo at ratio 0.79 on both reference wings. Hole radius is now ~22.6 mm.
- **Motor wiring, round 3 - belly entry NEAR THE MOTOR feeding an internal run**: the aft-bay belly port (round 2) was wrong twice over: the builder wanted the hole "near to where the motor mount is", and wanted the leads ENCLOSED from there - "to then lead inside the plane until it reaches the inside of the fuselage bay". `_motor_entry_run` walks forward from the mount to the aft-most station deep enough for the channel (widest tier + skin), drills the 6.5 mm entry vertically through the bottom skin into the channel's belly, and reuses `motor_conduit`'s router from that point - self-supporting gabled section, tier ladder, dive to the MEASURED cavity band, mouth 6 mm inside the void. Only the hop from the motor to the hole is external. Both cuts go through `cut_conduits` and the test classifies the entry bore's line through the skin as air.

- **The horn was rebuilt as a rounded, beveled triangle ANCHORED TO THE TRAILING EDGE (builder's decision, round 3)**: the builder asked three times, finally in caps - "at the REAR of the control surface - at the trailing edge of the wing, FAR FROM THE JOINT", rounded, beveled, hole near the tip with a safe rim. Recorded as a user decision so it is not "optimized" back toward the hinge. The base now sits `te_margin_mm` (2.5) short of the surface's MEASURED local trailing edge - measured by probing the solid station-by-station, because the old bounding-box reach belongs to the whole elevon and on a swept wing overhangs the real lip at the horn's own station. Two things had to be measured, not assumed. (1) A reflexed section's TE curls UP past the hinge-axis plane, so the old blade top fixed at z=0 would miss the surface entirely back there; the root now reads the local skin band by classification and fills the section to just under the upper skin - at the TE the root is effectively a rib, which is the bond a lever on a 1-2 mm skin needs. (2) A trailing-edge hole is intrinsically a long-radius, HIGH-RAKE horn, and rake is where the four-bar dies: the rake cap was scanned on both cached wings - 50 deg gives the best balanced throws (-18.5/+21.7 on the 610, -14.0/+20.1 on the 1200, for +/-45 of servo), at 55 the up-throw halves, and by 60 the linkage toggles and up-throw is gone entirely. So the hole depth grows to hold rake at 50 deg, giving hole radii ~44 mm (610) / ~73 mm (1200) against the 11 mm arm: ratio ~0.5-0.4, roughly 2-2.5x the holding torque of the old 22.6 mm horn, at the cost of throw - the trade the builder explicitly chose ("more torque"). The shape itself is the convex hull of the base segment and a tip circle of radius `hole_edge_mm` (4.0) centred EXACTLY on the 2.5 mm bore, so the rim is 2.75 mm everywhere around the hole by construction rather than by hoping a fillet lands right; the base corners carry 2.5 mm fillets and both faces a 0.6 mm chamfer (`bevel_mm`), which also kills elephant's-foot when the elevon prints skin-down. The chamfer is cosmetic and falls back to sharp edges if OCC refuses it. Bore-last + classify-open verification unchanged.

- **The trailing-edge lip is found by walking FORWARD from beyond the surface, not aft from the hinge**: the first `_local_reach` walked aft from s=2 and treated the first empty station as the lip. On the 1.2 m wing the horn's spanwise station happens to land exactly on the 20% hinge KNUCKLE, whose pin-clearance bore has no material anywhere near the axis - the walk died at its first probe, reported reach 0, and the fallback (85% of the bounding-box reach, which on a swept elevon belongs to a different station and runs 30 mm long) parked the horn base at 70-84 mm on a surface whose real lip is at ~72 mm. The blade hung off the back, the fuse gave two solids, and BOTH elevons shipped hornless - caught only because the fuse failure is warned and the bore existence check leaves `holes_cut` unset, exactly the class of silent loss the doctrine exists for. The walk now starts beyond the bounding box and comes FORWARD: past the lip there is guaranteed to be nothing, so the first material found IS the trailing edge, and no hinge-region geometry (knuckles, coves, bevels) is ever probed. The horn fuse also gained the `.copy()` retry the bay cut uses.

- **The solid classifier itself is a witness that can lie, so every skin probe is corroborated**: on the pytest swept fixture (900 x 1400 box), a cluster of phantom `BRepClass3d_SolidClassifier` hits BEYOND the trailing edge - near-tangent rays along the razor lip misreport, and ON counted as material - formed a fake "skin band" at h=+5 on the right elevon only. The max() across base samples believed it, parked the blade root 7 mm below the real underside, the blade never touched the surface, the fuse failed, and the elevon shipped hornless; the LEFT elevon of the same wing was fine, which is what betrayed it as noise rather than geometry. Probes now count only strict IN (never ON), a reach hit needs a second confirming probe 0.3 mm away, a skin band must be at least 3 contiguous samples (~1 mm) thick, and any band whose top strays more than 4 mm from the median of its neighbours is discarded before the aggregation. The bore/fuse existence checks caught the failure; the hardening makes the measurement itself trustworthy.

- **The motor-entry pre-check gates on the SMALLEST channel tier, not the widest**: `_motor_entry_run` refused the plank outright - "no station near the mount is deep enough" - because its depth gate demanded the connector tier (9 mm clear + walls) while the conduit router it hands off to degrades gracefully through four tiers down to a 2.6 mm cable channel that passes the three flat motor leads. A plank's aft body never reaches 15 mm of depth, so the widest-tier gate was a death sentence for the whole planform's motor wiring, discovered only when the per-planform regression finally ran against the belly-entry code (the swept-only subset had been standing in for it). The gate now asks for the floor tier and lets the router choose and report what actually fits; the walk also extends to the bay wall instead of stopping 90 mm out.

- **The motor wire run walks TOWARD THE BAY, not "forward"**: `_motor_entry_run` hard-coded the pusher picture - motor at the tail, bay ahead, walk forward. The plank flies a TRACTOR at the nose (x = 5 mm), so the walk marched straight off the front of the aircraft, every station off-chord, and the wiring was refused with the same "no station deep enough" message the depth gate uses - two different failures behind one reason string. The direction now comes from where the mount stands relative to the bay: a pusher's run goes forward into the bay's AFT wall (`x1_mm`), a tractor's goes aft into the bay's FORWARD wall (`x0_mm`), and `motor_conduit` already handled both directions (it takes the sign from `x_bay_aft - x_mount`) - only the entry-hole placement had the assumption baked in.
  Follow-up, same failure string, third cause: even walking the right way, the tractor run was refused because the fixed 15 mm bay standoff plus the fixed 8 mm first station consumed the whole 17 mm nose-to-bay gap. Both now scale with the gap (standoff 35%, first station 30%, with floors), and the plank routes: belly entry 6 mm aft of the nose mount, a short enclosed run through the bay's FORWARD wall, both cuts mesh-gated and the entry proven open - 5/5 plank tests pass. One reason string that covered three distinct failures (walked off the aircraft, gate too greedy, standoff too fat) cost three build-test cycles; refusals should name the numbers that produced them.

- **The horn is capped at 15 mm below the skin (builder's decision, round 4), and the cap - not the trailing edge - now sets the hole line**: "keep it like this, however make it shorter/stubbier. It only needs to be 15mm long max." The round-3 trailing-edge anchor and a 15 mm stub are physically incompatible: with the near-chordwise pushrod, torque comes from the hole's depth below the hinge axis, and a hole ~11 mm deep parked 35+ mm aft would sit at ~70 deg of rake - past the measured 60 deg toggle where up-throw is zero. So `protrude_max_mm` (15) bounds the hole depth, the 50 deg rake cap converts that to the furthest workable aft offset (depth x tan50, ~18 mm), and the base follows the hole; on a surface short enough for the trailing edge to be nearer than that, the TE anchor still governs. Everything else from round 3 survives unchanged - rounded tip lobe centred on the 2.5 mm bore (2.75 mm rim by construction), base fillets, face bevels, measured root band, bore-last verification. Measured result on both reference wings: hole ~18-19 mm aft, radius 23-25 mm, throws -25/+22 deg for +/-45 of servo at ratio ~0.8-0.86 - proper elevon throws, and a horn the size of a commercial micro horn (Du-Bro micro: 12.7 mm). The stub is honestly the better linkage than the deep TE horn (-18/+21 at ratio 0.51); the builder's instinct converged on what the four-bar wanted anyway.
  Follow-up: lowering the cheap depth gate exposed a fourth failure in the same function - the walk STOPPED at the first station that passed the gate, and when the router (the real authority, whose floor tier wants ~9.1 mm under a 2 mm skin against the gate's 8.6) refused that station, the whole run was refused: the 610 lost its motor wiring to a station 0.76 mm too shallow with metres of deeper body just forward of it. The walk now collects candidate stations and lets the router veto them one by one; the gate is a pre-filter, never the decision.

- **The elevon SIDE gaps are doubled to 1.0 mm per face (builder's decision); hinge gap and trailing edge stay at their derived values**: "double the tolerance/space only for the left and right side of both control panels." `end_clearance_mm` 0.50 -> 1.00. These are the two spanwise end faces where the freeing cut leaves the elevon coincident with the wing (0.0000 mm measured before trimming) - broad flat parallel faces, the worst welding geometry on the plate - and unlike the hinge gap they do no aerodynamic sealing work, so opening them costs nothing except 2 mm of elevon span (~0.5% - below anything the aero model resolves). Nothing else moves: hinge stations stand `end_clearance_mm + axial_clearance_mm` inboard of the trim planes by construction, so they follow the wider trim automatically. Measured on the built 610 wing: 1.10 mm of free air at all four end faces (probe quantization on a 1.00 design gap), captive-pin and horn tests green.

- **The part colour key went monochrome (builder's decision): white structure, grey moving surfaces, darker-grey hatch; red stays on the CG marker**: was blue/green/orange. Applied in BOTH places that carry it - `api.ROLE_COLORS` (the preview manifest and the on-screen legend) and `exporters._PART_COLORS` (the STEP assembly) - so the model looks the same in the browser and in CAD. The two greys are deliberately DIFFERENT (mid vs dark): "control" and "hatch" both read as grey, per the builder's palette, but a lid that vanished into the elevons would defeat the point of a colour key.

- **The servo envelope is now the builder's OWN measured servo, at 0.5 mm per face - because the test print proved the datasheet envelope wrong in the one place no datasheet draws**: the printed pocket rejected a real HXT900. The builder supplied tahustvedt's measured HXT900 model (thingiverse.com/thing:1251554) as the reference; slicing it triangle-exact found the killer: the real part has a FULL-WIDTH gear-head housing standing 4.4 mm above the case top (23.2 -> 27.4 mm, 14.6 mm long, spanning the spline end), where the FS90 drawing - the only manufacturer drawing for this case - shows just a 6 mm output boss. The old pocket relieved that zone with a hub-sized, HALF-DEPTH patch, so the head hit the pocket floor before the ears ever reached their recess: mechanical interference, not a tolerance problem, which is why raising clearances alone would never have fixed it. Changes: all case/ear/spline dims re-measured off the STL (body 22.8 x 11.8, case top 23.2 incl. its 0.2 lip, head 27.4, spline 29.8 total, ears 32.4 span at 16.2-18.5, hole pitch 27.1, spline 5.9 from the near end, plus the 1.0 mm moulded lead-grommet stub past the shaft-side end face which now offsets that end wall); a fourth full-depth "head" pocket region over the measured head footprint; `SERVO_CLEARANCE_MM` 0.35 -> 0.50 (builder's spec). Verified the only way that means anything: every one of the 32,421 vertices of the builder's actual servo STL, posed as `servo_bay` poses the servo, has >= 0.49 mm of air to the pocket walls - worst points sit exactly at the designed clearance. Location, orientation and position of the servo are unchanged, per the builder.

- **Wire runs got belled mouths, a deeper pocket overshoot, and the motor channel a raised floor (builder feedback: obstructed, undersized)**: three changes. (1) Both conduit routers now flare the cross-section 1.5x over the last 15% of each end - the openings into the servo pocket and the equipment bay are funnels, not tight-fitting holes, and any residual kink from the endpoint-height clamp is swallowed by extra free area. The flare runs through the same window fitting as the base tier, so a mouth that would break the skin fails the tier honestly. (2) The servo run's start overshoot rose 2 -> 6 mm, driving the end cap deep into the pocket cavity so it is a face in free air rather than a ledge flush with the pocket wall. (3) The motor conduit's 4.4 mm pull-string floor tier is GONE - its floor is now 5.6 mm, matched to the servo channel's cable tier, per the builder ("needs to be the same size as the servo's at least"); if 5.6 does not fit the run refuses and names the numbers. The `_motor_entry_run` depth gate follows the new floor; the router-veto retry already covers any gate/router disagreement.

- **The servo pocket is the measured servo's INVERSE at 0.25 mm (builder's decision, round 3, superseding the 0.5 mm box fit)**: "re-reference the STL ... add a 0.25mm clearance around that whole model and implement that inverse shape." Each measured feature - case, lead grommet stub (now its own small relief at Z_s 1-7 instead of padding the whole end wall), mounting ears, gear head, spline - is a vertical prism offset by exactly 0.25 mm, so the servo still drops in from below and the cavity hugs the shape everywhere else. Verified against the builder's STL: all 32,421 vertices sit at 0.24-0.25 mm of air - the pocket IS the model plus a quarter millimetre. Noted for the record: 0.25 is below the 0.35 mm printed-XY separability band, so this is a snug press-in fit by design; the builder test prints and owns the call. The horn hub's swing-room boss patch survives (it clears the HORN, which is not part of the servo's own shape).

- **Every wire run is ONE ROUND PIPE, 8.25 mm diameter, everywhere (builder's decision, round 3)**: "no fancy shape, just a circle and circular cross section ... equivalent to doing a 'pipe' or 'loft' function in Fusion 360 ... 0 obstruction at all." The gabled-teardrop tiers (servo: connector/edgewise/cable/tight; motor: bullets/16awg/18awg) are all replaced by a single circular 8.25 mm tier, the conduit sweep lofts true circles perpendicular to the path, the round belly entry hole grew 6.5 -> 8.25 to match, the servo pocket's own lead stub grew 5.6 x 4.2 -> 8.5 x 8.5 so nothing at the pocket junction is narrower than the pipe, and the round-2 belled mouths default OFF (a flare is a diameter change; the builder wants to sight one circle straight through). The builder wrote both "at least 8.25" and "8.15" in the same message - resolved to 8.25, the value that satisfies both readings as a floor. Two engineering notes recorded with the decision rather than argued: (1) a horizontal circular channel's crown is an unsupported overhang past 45 deg, which is exactly why the teardrop existed - on an H2D in PLA an 8 mm horizontal bore bridges with some crown sag but prints; the builder owns the surface finish inside the pipe. (2) There is no smaller fallback tier any more, so a panel too shallow for 8.25 + walls refuses its wire run and names the numbers instead of quietly shipping a thinner channel.

- **The pipe mouths are trumpeted and the pocket's rectangular lead slot is gone (builder, round 4: "0 sharp corners or edges ... the transition at all")**: the round 8.25 mm bore is constant through the run and widens tangentially (smoothstep loft, 1.6x over the last 15%) into each opening, so where it breaks into the servo pocket, the belly skin and the equipment bay the wire meets a funnel rather than a rim - this walks back the round-3 "no flare" reading of "just a circle", because the builder's newer instruction is about the TRANSITIONS, and a tangent-continuous trumpet is the only way a bore can meet a wall with no edge. The boxy open-to-the-skin cable slot at the pocket's lead end (5.6 x 4.2, later 8.5 x 8.5) is deleted outright: the pipe's own trumpeted mouth now reaches 6 mm into the pocket cavity, and a rectangular side channel would only add back the exact corners being removed. `cable_stub_mm` survives as an override.

- **The horn stands straight relative to the WING, not along the hinge sweep (builder's request)**: the blade used to be extruded and bored along the hinge axis, so on a swept wing its faces sat ~26 deg off the pushrod's plane and the rod met the horn at that angle. It is now extruded and bored along WORLD y - square to the servo arm's swing plane - while every piece of lever geometry (root-setback swing clearance, rake cap, hole radius) is still computed perpendicular to the TRUE hinge axis and converted to chordwise mm by 1/cos(sweep). The linkage solver needed no change at all: it projects the hole onto the hinge frame with dot products, so the perpendicular lever arm comes out identical - measured on both reference wings, radii and throws unchanged to the millimetre (23.2 mm, -25.0/+26.2 on the 610), rod stretch 0.03/0.14 mm over the full throw. The foul check still sweeps about the true hinge axis.
  Follow-up: the first trumpet implementation treated the flare as load-bearing in the window fitter, and since the fitter cannot know that a mouth opens into an already-carved pocket (it reads the wing's crown/keel, not the cavities), it refused BOTH servo pipes on the 610 - while every test stayed green, because nothing asserted the conduit cuts exist. Two fixes: the flare now shrinks per-station toward the base pipe wherever the local section cannot clear it (kept monotone toward each mouth, so what remains is always a smooth funnel and the Ø8.25 bore itself is never compromised), and `test_every_fitted_servo_gets_its_wire_pipe` now makes a missing pipe a test failure - the fourth instance of the "operation whose absence matters needs its own existence check" doctrine.

## v2 — multi-type (2026-08-12/13)

- **The app grew back multi-type support (builder's decision, reversing their own flying-wing-only order)**: "recreate that feature where we can choose other options besides flying wing designs." `airplane_type` selects the family — `flying_wing` (v1, tagged `v1` on GitHub, byte-identical behavior guarded by a regression test) and `conventional` — plus an `"any"` request mode that optimizes across types from bare parameters and returns five variants spanning both. Conventional code lives BESIDE the flying-wing code (`stability_conv.py`, `physics/conventional.py`, `cad/conventional.py`), never inside it; dispatch happens at exactly four public seams. The hardware doctrine (measured HXT900 inverse pocket at 0.25 mm, Ø8.25 round pipes with trumpeted mouths, print-in-place hinges, world-aligned 15 mm horns, existence-checked cuts) carried over verbatim — it is about printers and servos, not about tails.
- **Conventional stability is genuinely tailed math, not the flying-wing checks with new numbers**: tail-volume sizing (V_H 0.40–0.65, V_V 0.025–0.05), NP as the lift-slope-weighted AC of wing+tail with a Munk fuselage destabilization term, decalage trim, static margin 0.05–0.15 — every band a cited row of RESEARCH_CONVENTIONAL.md. The two V_V checks must never share code: the flying-wing fin legitimately reports far below the tail-aft band (fin sits ~one root chord behind CG) and that asymmetry is documented on both sides.
- **The elevator is two panels, the rudder is built in a rotated frame**: a one-piece elevator would slice through the fin root and tail post, so it splits left/right with the centreline left structural. The rudder's hinge is near-vertical, but all the hinge/horn machinery reasons in a horizontal frame — so the airframe+rudder pair rotates +90° about x, gets hinged and horned there, and rotates back. Elevator/rudder servos sit in the fuselage bay (wing-mounted aileron servos keep the v1 pocket); their pushrod exits and all wire runs are the same Ø8.25 pipes, existence-checked.
- **The five conventional styles mirror the five flying-wing characters** (`CONV_STYLES`: trainer/sport/aerobatic/floater/speed), and like planforms they are the VARIANT axis, not a request field: every conventional generate solves all five and the sidebar's Style select chooses which variant leads the gallery. Wiring a hard style constraint into `GenerateRequest` was deliberately deferred — it would touch the schema all types share.
- **CAD moved to worker processes with a byte-identity proof** (`cadjobs.py`): OCC's GIL made concurrent previews serialize (five requests, zero finished in 11.5 min). A 2-worker spawn pool now builds them (all five in ~11.5 min, first at ~3.7 min), with the same builders and tolerances — the calibration STL hashes identical through both paths, and `AEROFORGE_CAD_WORKERS=0` restores inline builds. Physics stays sequential on purpose: the five variant solves share `ctx["seeds"]`, so parallelizing them would change numeric output — forbidden by the perf contract (docs/PERF_NOTES.md).

- **The wire pipe starts AT the lead grommet, not merely near the pocket (builder's decision, v2, from the printed part)**: "the wire managment hole ... shifted/reangled so it starts at the edge of the servo where the wire to control it is (the same side where the horn attaches) ... rather than me twisting it back along the bottom of the servo." The pipe's pocket mouth was centred half a channel-width INSIDE the case's end face (x_c + body/2 - cw/2) at the case-bottom wall - a ~5 mm forward offset from the measured grommet, which on the printed wing forced exactly the fold-back the builder describes. The default `cable_exit` x now stands at the middle of the measured grommet itself (case end face + clearance + half the 1.0 mm stub protrusion), inside the stub relief the pocket already carves, so the trumpeted Ø13 mouth opens around the grommet and the wire runs straight off it into the bore. y and z were already right: the 6 mm start overshoot lands the cap across the grommet's measured height band (Z_s 1-7). Measured on the built 610 wing: cap 1.11 mm aft of the grommet centre (the route holds a chord FRACTION over the overshoot, so the cap drifts aft with sweep - in line with the wire, which leaves the grommet chordwise), and every classification probe down the first 25 mm of bore is air. Both types inherit the fix through `servo_bay` (the conventional aileron run starts at the same `cable_exit`), and both pipe existence tests now also assert the mouth is within 2.5 mm of the grommet - the old fold-back position must keep failing.

## v3 — eight types + configuration axes (2026-08-16/17)

- **Eight types, four configuration axes (builder's brief)**: conventional, flying wing, delta, canard, tandem, biplane, twin-boom and glider, with tractor/pusher, single/twin motor, V-tail/conventional/T-tail and high/mid/low wing offered as their OWN choices rather than baked into each type. "The current servo and wiring management systems are good to go, keep these across all designs" — the hardware doctrine carried over verbatim again, as it did into v2. The axes ride a `design["config"]` block whose absent keys mean "this type's researched default", so a v2 request produces v2 output unchanged. Bands and reference airframes are cited per type in `RESEARCH_TYPES_V3.md`; the working contract is `docs/V3_PLAN.md`.
- **A delta is a flying-wing dict, not a new topology**: `planform: "delta"` rides the v1 tailless CAD unmodified (low AR, 45–60° LE sweep, centre fin, elevons). Only one thing had to change to admit it, and it was a REPORTING bug the delta merely exposed: `airframe_extents_m` evaluated a centre fin's height at the fin's own trailing edge, but the builder SLIDES a centre fin forward of a pusher nacelle for prop-disc clearance, where the crown is higher and the root band digs deeper — the recorded height came out 5.4 mm short. The extents now evaluate at the slid station whenever the mount is a pusher. Tractor centre fins (the v1 plank) are untouched and their numbers do not move.
- **The delta's servo-pipe mouth tolerance scales with sweep, and only aft**: the flying-wing suite bounds the pipe mouth within 2.5 mm of the measured lead grommet, calibrated at ≤26° of sweep. The route holds a chord FRACTION over its start overshoot, so the cap drifts AFT along the wire's own direction as sweep grows (measured 1.11 mm at 26°). A 45–60° delta drifts further along that same line, so the aft allowance is `2.5 + 6·tan(sweep)` while the forward bound stays hard at 2.5 mm — the old fold-back mouth (5 mm FORWARD) must keep failing.
- **Type dispatch is a table, not a chain, and it is tested**: `cad/geometry.py` resolves `airplane_type` through one `_TYPE_MODULES` map that both public entry points share. `cad/multiwing.py` was finished and never wired in, so canard, tandem and biplane would have built as FLYING WINGS from the server while every per-type CAD suite passed — those suites import their builder module directly, by design, which is exactly the gap. `tests/test_type_dispatch.py` now asserts the registry and the dispatch table cannot drift apart, that each type reaches its owning module, and that an absent `airplane_type` is still the v1 flying wing.
- **A fuse is only believed when the feature can be CLASSIFIED in the result (`geometry.fuse_feature`)**: the biplane shipped with no vertical fin at all. `base.fuse(fin)` returned a shape that was `isValid()` and exactly one solid — the two flags every builder tested — and simply did not contain the fin. Nothing downstream noticed: the rudder split then took an 89 mm³ chip out of the tail cone and called it a rudder, its horn failed to fuse to that chip, and the meta reported "2 fins, fused: true". The gate now requires the result to contain the addition's bounding box and a witness point classified inside each operand, and a feature that still cannot be fused says so by name in the warnings instead of vanishing. Deliberately GEOMETRIC, never volumetric: `Volume()` on these spline-heavy solids is computed to a loose default tolerance and disagreed with itself by 32,000 mm³ on a shape that classified perfectly, so a volume test would fail good fuses and pass bad ones. The same check replaced the three copies of `_fuse_in` (multiwing, conventional, twinboom) so it cannot be fixed in one module and missed in another. This is the fifth instance of the doctrine that an operation whose absence matters gets its own existence check, after the tessellation gate, the horn bores, the conduit runs and the bay ladder.
- **A control surface never carries a fixed vertical surface**: the canard's aileron freeing pocket is unbounded in z (correctly — nothing sits behind that wing), but the TIP FINS stand on that same wing, and at the sized 0.95 outer station the pocket reached straight up through the fin and cut its whole aft section free. The "aileron" came off the build 108,907 mm³ and 164.7 mm tall, on a wing whose skin tops out at 20 mm — two thirds of a fin hinged to a roll control. The aileron now ends inboard of the fin foot (fin half-thickness + 1.0 mm spanwise end clearance + 3 mm of wing to carry the root moment), which is where a tip-finned aeroplane puts it anyway, in BOTH build modes so the one-piece scribe line agrees with the split; the station it actually got is recorded as `ailerons_built` rather than silently standing in for the sized one. Same family as the biplane's struts, which were already footed clear of the aileron hinge line for this reason.
- **The servo pocket's TOP SLOT is 1.5 mm longer, and the axis is the whole lesson (`servos.BOSS_SEAT_EXTRA_MM`)**: the builder test-printed and reported "the top of the servo ... needs to be increased by 1.5mm; the very top of the servo is not pushing in all the way". The first attempt read "top" as the boss patch's DEPTH and added 1.5 mm to `boss_up` (into the skin, along Y_s) — reasoning that the boss patch is the shallowest in the pocket at 10.40 mm against the case's 12.05, so the tallest feature sat on the shallowest floor. That reasoning was self-consistent and completely wrong, and the builder checked the output in Fusion against the old models and found no difference — because the change was real but on an axis nobody was binding on. The feature they were measuring is the pocket's top slot: from the gear-head housing's top (`head_h + cl` = 27.65) to the boss region's top (`total_h + cl + 1.0` = 31.05), **3.40 mm of Z_s**, the only stretch of the pocket that is boss and nothing else — "there is 3.4mm gap where the servo/motor movement is". It now runs to 32.55, a 4.90 mm slot, and the depth bump was reverted. Verified where the first attempt was not: the cutter solid gains 1,134 mm³ and 1.50 mm of bbox, and in a BUILT airframe the pocket's top end wall moves from y=248.0297 to y=249.5297 — 1.5000 mm, measured by point classification, not inferred from a parameter. Station, roof and the inboard walk are unchanged on all four planforms. **A dimension is not a change until it is measured in the built solid**: the same doctrine as the tessellation gate, the horn bores and `fuse_feature`, applied this time to a number the builder could see and I could not."
- **A spanwise STATION is not a clearance — clear a fixed surface against the pocket's real boundary, not against the hinge line**: the first fix for the above pulled the aileron's outer station in to `y_fin − fin_foot_half − 4 mm` and the aileron came off carrying the whole fin anyway (z = 164.7 mm again, on a 23.8 mm crown). `_separate_elevons` bounds the freeing pocket with a plane whose normal is the HINGE LINE direction, and the hinge line is the 75%-chord line, not the leading edge: on a tapered wing that line is usually swept FORWARD even when the LE is swept back — measured **−7.58°** on `canard_sport`. So the pocket's outboard edge rakes OUTBOARD as it runs aft, and over the tip fin's 167 mm chordwise footprint it reached y = 611.4 mm against a fin face at 591.4 — 20 mm inside the fin, from a station that was itself correctly computed. The station is now solved (`multiwing._outer_frac_clear_of`, bisection on `_elevon_hinge_line`, no booleans so it is free) so that **every corner of the fin's plan footprint sits outboard of the actual bounding plane** by the 4 mm gap: 0.930 → 0.8928 semispan, pocket max y 587.3, aileron still 217 mm. The footprint is taken at the fin's most-aft height, not its root, because the pocket is unbounded in z and a swept fin's tip trailing edge is its worst point. The general lesson is the one the tessellation gate and `fuse_feature` already taught in their own domains: **verify the property you actually want against the geometry that will be built, never against the parameter you fed it.** A station is an input; clearance is an outcome.
- **The check must know what the design legitimately contains — an asymmetric elevator pair is CORRECT here**: the test above compared every moving surface to the MAIN WING's crown, which on a biplane means measuring a tail elevator against a lower wing 46 mm below it; that is fixed (each surface is now checked against the host it is actually hinged to). But the failure it produced sent this session down a wrong path worth recording. `elevator_right` reached 13.7 mm below the stab keel while `elevator_left` stopped clean, and the asymmetry read as the signature of a boolean reaching where nothing bounded it — the tail-cone-chip failure the biplane had already produced once. It is not. **It is the control horn**: `tip_below_skin_mm = 15.0` against `protrude_max_mm = 15.0`, the user's own cap, and only ONE panel of a split elevator is horned because the two are joined by a wire at the bench (standard practice, and what `cad/conventional.py` already documents). A `z_min` floor added to `_separate_surfaces_bounded` on that false premise changed the output by exactly nothing — which is the evidence that settled it — and was reverted rather than left in looking like a fix. Two lessons: **a symmetry argument is only as good as your inventory of what is deliberately asymmetric**, and a bound that provably changes no geometry is not a harmless safety net, it is a false record of a bug that was never there. The check now allows the horn cap downward and stays tight upward, which is the only direction the fin-on-elevon failure can take — a fin stands UP.

## Servo change — HXT900 → SG90 (2026-08-19)

- **The servo is now the TowerPro SG90, and NOTHING else about the hardware doctrine moved (builder's decision)**: "Instead of using the HXT900 servo motor, we will be using the SG90 which is more widely available ... Keep the exact same tolerances, wire management, and orientation as the current servo, we are just replacing the size/shape." Availability is the whole reason; the two are the same 9 g class. `SERVO_HXT900` is renamed `SERVO_SG90` and re-measured triangle-exact off the builder's own `SG90-NEW-SERVO/SG90.stl`, feature by feature, exactly as the HXT900 model was. Everything the servo does NOT determine is untouched: `SERVO_CLEARANCE_MM` stays 0.25 per face, `BOSS_SEAT_EXTRA_MM` stays 1.5, the Ø8.25 mm round pipes and their 1.6× trumpeted mouths are unchanged, the pipe still starts AT the lead grommet, the horn still carries one 2.5 mm bore under a 15 mm cap, and the servo still lies on its side with the shaft axis SPANWISE. The physics did not move either: `weights.SERVO_MASS` was already 0.009 kg, which is the SG90's own 9 g.
- **What actually changed is the envelope, and the one number that matters is the THIN axis**: `body_wid_mm` 11.8 → **12.50 mm**, so every servo pocket in every type is 0.70 mm deeper into the skin than it was. That is the dominant consequence of the swap — the pocket-depth axis is the one `servo_bay` searches on, so a station that only just roofed an HXT900 now walks inboard, and a section that cannot roof 12.50 + 0.25 + wall refuses and says so. The rest is small: case 23.2 → 22.35, gear head 27.4 → 26.65 (still full-footprint, still 4.30 mm proud of the case top — the FS90 drawing's boss-only picture is still wrong), spline top 29.8 → 29.37, spline Ø4.8 → 4.92 at 6.00 mm from the near end, ears 32.4 → 32.45 span and 2.3 → 2.65 thick with their mid-plane at 16.875.   The fix is the one ARCHITECTURE.md states for this case — widen the budget in the optimizer, never clamp the part. `evaluate_twinboom` now computes the smallest local chord whose section can hold the pocket and lifts the chord (by capping AR) until the station at the aileron root clears it. Three things had to be right, and each was measured rather than assumed:
  (1) **The pocket is a BOX, so what the section owes it is min(crown) − max(keel) across the whole 32.95 mm footprint, not peak thickness.** On a NACA 2412 that well is ~0.89 of maximum thickness; on the cambered 4412 that `endurance` and `mapper` fly it is less, which is why those two stayed broken through an aspect-ratio sweep that gave `endurance` a 158 mm chord — *bigger* than the 151 mm chord `trainer` was passing on. **Chord alone does not predict it; the section shape does.** A first pass that lowered the AR bands by 6% was measured, found to fix nothing reliably (the optimizer simply re-chose span), and abandoned.
  (2) **A world-vertical pocket in a tilted wing loses depth at its corners.** Dihedral tilts the panel across the pocket's own 30.87 mm of span and twist tilts the section across its 32.95 mm of chord: on an ordinary 2°/2° wing that is 1.08 + 0.65 = **1.73 mm**, more than the whole SG90/HXT900 difference. Aspect ratio is essentially untouched (−1.2% to +1.8% on the three that grew); the optimizer answered by growing SPAN, not by collapsing AR.

That case is left to refuse honestly rather than pushed below the cited band — but it is now **recorded** (`geometry.servo_chord_floor.fits`) instead of only appearing in a CAD warning nobody reads, and the test asserts a miss can only ever happen with AR already on the floor.

- **The bay cut can DELETE the compartment floor, and no gate was watching (builder's report, 2026-08-21: "a big hole in the bottom of the design, in the main fuselage. just no wall at all, just a large box hole going through the design")**: their swept-sport generation (box 700×1200×300, 17 m/s) shipped its `centre_body` part with a **224 × 64 mm void clean through the belly** under the equipment bay — while the one-piece STL of the same design was perfect, which is why every scan of the one-piece previews found nothing.
